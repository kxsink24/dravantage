## Mission and Mandate

*What the organization exists to do, and the official commitments that currently govern how it operates (legislation, accreditation, board directives, grant conditions).*

*Why this matters: The mission is the lens through which every recommendation gets filtered. A recommendation that advances the mission lands; one that doesn't gets deprioritized regardless of technical merit. Knowing the mandate also surfaces non-negotiable constraints — compliance requirements or funding conditions that are barriers before they're opportunities.*

## Strategic Priorities (12–24 month horizon)

*What leadership has committed to publicly or internally — growth targets, transformation programs, operational goals.*

*Why this matters: The DRA's job is to show how data readiness connects to what the customer is already trying to achieve. If you can trace a specific barrier directly to a strategic priority the sponsor already owns, you don't need to convince them data matters — you just need to show them what's in the way.*

## Compelling Event

*The specific pressure making this DRA happen now — not eventually, now. Deadline, mandate, leadership change, budget cycle, competitive threat, or prior failed initiative.*

*Why this matters: The compelling event is what turns a "we should do this someday" conversation into a scheduled engagement. It should appear in the kickoff framing and in the executive summary. If you can't articulate it in one sentence, you haven't found it yet.*

## Magic Wand Responses

*How stakeholders answered "If data were no longer a constraint, what would you do differently?" — verbatim and paraphrased.*

*Why this matters: Magic wand responses reveal the target state in the customer's own language. They're more revealing than any question about current-state problems because they bypass the learned helplessness that most data-constrained organizations develop. The answers also vary by persona — Leaders want strategic outcomes, Business users want operational speed, IT wants engineering clarity. Those differences are worth noting.*

## Sponsorship

*The three-legged stool: Business sponsor (owns the outcome), IT sponsor (controls the platform), Funding sponsor (controls the budget). May be the same person in small orgs.*

*Why this matters: A DRA without all three sponsors is at risk. A recommendation that the Business sponsor champions but IT hasn't bought into will stall in implementation. A recommendation IT loves but has no budget line is a slide deck. Documenting who fills each role — and any gaps — helps the SE know which conversations still need to happen.*
