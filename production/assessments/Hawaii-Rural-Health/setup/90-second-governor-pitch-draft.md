# 90-Second Governor / Marc Benioff Video Pitch

## Draft voiceover

> **Governor Green, rural healthcare can look stable on paper.** But critical handoffs—referrals, eligibility, claims, provider coordination—can still break. The data exists; the challenge is seeing the right signal in time to act.
>
> Imagine a governed view across Hawaii’s existing systems: connect the right data, protect it, and put a clear signal in front of the person accountable for the next action. **Connect. Trust. Act.**
>
> For a rural resident, that could mean linking a care-transition or eligibility signal to the right provider or community resource before a missed handoff becomes a crisis. We are not proposing to replace the HIE or rearchitect the state—just a focused, measurable starting point.
>
> That starts with executive alignment: one outcome, one sponsor, one operational owner, and the data we can use now. Then a focused readiness sprint with DOH, SHPDA, Med-QUEST, HHIE, and providers.
>
> Most programs stall not because the vision is wrong, but because priorities, ownership, and data readiness are unclear. Our offer is simple: assess readiness, show where Hawaii can start safely, define measures, and build from an early win. Marc, this is a chance to prove what a trusted platform can do when it starts with Hawaii’s outcomes. Governor, the question is not whether Hawaii has a vision. It’s where we start.

## Timing

Approximately 210 words, targeting about 90 seconds at a measured executive pace.

## Story spine

1. **The tension:** Critical handoffs can fail even when information exists.
2. **The vision:** A governed view connects data to the person accountable for action.
3. **The human outcome:** A rural resident reaches the right provider or resource earlier.
4. **The low-friction move:** Complement the HIE and existing systems; do not lead with rearchitecture.
5. **The ask:** Align on one outcome, sponsor, operational owner, and usable data set.
6. **The close:** Assess readiness, define measures, and build from an early win.

## Proposed visual beats

- 0–15 sec: Hawaii map, rural communities, and a simple critical-handoff visual.
- 15–30 sec: Existing systems and organizations connected through a governed view; avoid dense architecture.
- 30–50 sec: One rural resident journey from signal to provider/community action.
- 50–70 sec: One practical starting point—RHTP grants, provider readiness, or another validated outcome.
- 70–90 sec: Executive alignment, readiness sprint, measurable first pilot, and closing question.

## Recommendations applied from the latest canvas and Werner/Alexandre prep

- The canvas timing adds to 120 seconds, not 90. This revision is approximately 210 words and is designed for a measured 90-second delivery.
- Replaced “silent breakdowns” and “fall through the cracks” with “critical handoffs” so the message stays urgent without sounding accusatory or implying unverified customer failure.
- Replaced “bring the data into one place” with “a governed view across existing systems” to avoid implying a wholesale rearchitecture or state ownership of all clinical data.
- Removed the unsupported “90% of AI initiatives fail” statistic. The pitch now makes the same readiness point without introducing a fact-checking distraction.
- Made the executive ask explicit: align on one outcome, one sponsor, one operational owner, and the data that can be used now.
- Kept the architecture in service of the outcome. Product names and technical detail remain out of the first 90 seconds unless the audience asks for them.

## Claims and choices to validate before recording

- Confirm whether the intended audience is Governor Josh Green, Marc Benioff, or both in the same send.
- Confirm the exact ask: authorization for a readiness sprint, introduction to named state leaders, or approval of a specific pilot.
- Confirm whether “RHTP grants, provider readiness, and community needs” is the selected first use case or only the current recommendation.
- Confirm the preferred wording for Native Hawaiian communities and other priority populations with the account/customer team.
- Keep product names and architecture jargon out of the first 90 seconds unless the sender explicitly wants a technology-forward version.

## Grounding notes

This draft reflects the internal Hawaii team-sync direction to use a path-of-least-resistance, citizen/outcome-centered story; the Werner/Alexandre prep direction to create a concise executive pitch; the Slack positioning that existing systems should be complemented rather than replaced; and the public-research recommendation to begin with grants/compliance or provider-readiness outcomes. The first customer-side sponsor and exact pilot remain unconfirmed.
