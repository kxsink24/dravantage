## Executive thesis

The briefing frames Hawaii as being at the intersection of a large RHTP funding cycle and the planned CMS AHEAD model. It argues that a top-down clinical-data rearchitecture would face institutional fatigue and governance friction. It recommends a bounded entry point focused on RHTP grants administration, compliance visibility, and provider-network coordination, with broader population-health and data-platform work following once ownership and accountability are established. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]

## Public-record findings summarized by the document

### RHTP and AHEAD context

- The document reports a first-year Hawaii RHTP award of approximately $188.9M and a multi-year performance period extending into the early 2030s. It links the funding to rural access, telehealth, workforce, emergency services, interoperability, and value-based payment readiness. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- It describes the RHTP/AHEAD intersection as a compelling timing signal, with RHTP planning and procurement work preceding anticipated AHEAD global-budget and advanced-primary-care changes. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- The document identifies six RHTP initiative families: RHIN, Pili Ola, RICA, HOME RUN, Rural Respite Network, and RVBI. The initiative descriptions and funding figures require primary-source confirmation before external use. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]

### Organization and accountability map

- SHPDA is presented as a central health-planning, capacity, Certificate-of-Need, and RHTP coordination body under DOH. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- Med-QUEST's Health Analytics Program is presented as the operational owner/steward for the APCD/IDAP claims-data environment, while SHPDA retains related statutory and coordination responsibilities. This split should be validated directly because it is central to accountabilities and data ownership. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- HHIE is described as the clinical-exchange bridge, with University of Hawaii and community-based organizations participating in research, workforce, data, and last-mile service delivery. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]

### Data and interoperability signals

- The briefing describes a bifurcated landscape: HHIE/Health eNet for clinical exchange and Med-QUEST HAP/IDAP for claims-based analytics. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- It cites provider-adoption and manual-entry concerns, patient identity/data-quality work, and the need to bridge clinical, claims, SDoH, and provider-network data. These are public-research signals, not customer-confirmed current-state facts. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- The document names a technology landscape that includes InterSystems Health eNet, 4medica, Snowflake, Optum Symmetry, Tableau, Infor/EFS, Unite Us, and Salesforce in other Hawaii public-sector contexts. DOH/SHPDA-specific footprint and procurement status remain validation questions. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]

### Governance and execution signals

- The briefing cites historical IDAP/APCD IV&V findings involving delayed business rules, unclear roles, multiple vendors, competing work-management tools, schedule slippage, and budget/change-control pressure. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- It frames governance, ownership, privacy, PII handling, HIPAA/MARS-E, and cross-vendor accountability as the primary transformation risk—not merely a technical integration problem. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- It identifies a need for lightweight governance, visible compliance tracking, rapid deployment, and a clear accountable owner rather than another large bespoke multi-vendor program. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]

## Candidate first-use-case shortlist from the document

1. **RHTP sub-grant lifecycle and compliance visibility** — track applicants, awards, milestones, disbursements, and federal reporting using administrative/financial data first.
2. **RVBI virtual CIN/MSO provider relationship management** — track provider onboarding, credentialing, coaching, readiness, and support for AHEAD-related risk adoption.
3. **Community Care Coordination Hub** — connect clinical transitions with community resources and medical respite; higher governance and interoperability risk.
4. **APCD/IDAP data-governance and metadata workflow** — route definitions, access requests, stewardship, and approvals; high relevance but high political/vendor complexity.

These are candidate hypotheses, not approved solution scope. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]

## DRA implications

- The strongest convergence with the internal sync is a **business-first, bounded entry point**: grants/compliance visibility or provider-network readiness before attempting a statewide clinical-data platform conversation. [[inputs/interviews/2026-08-10-team-sync]] [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- The document strengthens the case for a potential **Trust Gap** and **Time Trap** hypothesis: governance and role ambiguity, plus documented program-management friction, may delay action even where data and funding exist. Confidence remains medium-low until customer-side evidence is collected. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- The document adds a potential **Blind Spot** around the cross-agency operating model: the DRA must determine who owns the outcome, who owns the data, who operates the workflow, and who controls funding. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
- The first customer conversation should validate operational ownership, current grants workflow, RHTP sub-award reporting, provider-network readiness, data-sharing boundaries, and the definition of a successful first pilot. [[inputs/customer-docs/2026-08-13-hawaii-rhtp-executive-briefing]]
